I’m a fifth-year PhD Candidate at the University of Michigan, where I work with Dallas Card and David Jurgens. My work focuses on applying Natural Language Processing and other computational tools to better understand media, communication, and politics. This includes analyzing the development of large media stories across hundreds of outlets (ACL Findings 2023), mapping the guest networks created by appearances on millions of podcasts (ACL 2025), and reviewing techniques to measure similarity between two people in a conversation (ACL 2025). I'm planning to earn my PhD in Spring 2026 and am currently on the job market looking for post-docs and research positions relating to NLP, political science, and media. If you’d like to get in touch, you can email me at blitt@umich.edu !

Active projects:

My current work focuses explicitly on how politicians communicate in alternative media:

  • Have conservatives truly won the podcast ecosystem?

    We're mapping where U.S. politicians have appeared, with a particular focus on the ideological slant and popularity of these podcasts. My particular interest here is understanding whether rumors of conservative dominance are accurate, and connecting partisan dynamics within the podcast space to more established formats such as radio and television.

  • Is tough journalism disincentivized?

    This project focuses on measuring not just the prevalence of journalists challenging politicians on podcasts, but also the consequences of this behavior over time. While both parties may accuse the other side of media bias, I'm interested in trying to understand whether "softball-question" interviewing is incentivized with regard to guest retention, audience size, and ratings.
